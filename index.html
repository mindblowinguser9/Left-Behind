LEFT BEHIND

An Interactive Archive of Humanity's Footprints Beyond Earth

Since the 1960s, NASA missions have left hardware on the Moon and Mars. Some of it was left on purpose. Some was thrown away during landing. Some simply stopped working. Each piece has a story, and the science it made possible.

The Stories

Three short stories, one for each object. Every fact comes from the sources listed below.

Why It Matters

These machines are more than leftovers. Each one is evidence of how people and robots explored other worlds.

Engineering

Real missions test real hardware. Opportunity worked about 60 times longer than planned. NASA reported that the Perseverance parachute showed no signs of damage from supersonic inflation. A JPL engineer called the landing the best-documented in Mars history, so engineers can study exactly what happened.

Scientific discovery

The Apollo rovers gave crews range, and NASA says the major discoveries of Apollo 15, 16 and 17 would not have been possible without them. Orbiters such as LRO can photograph old sites decades later. NASA's JPL says Opportunity's exploration laid the groundwork for future Mars missions.

History of exploration

Some hardware was left on purpose, some was designed to be discarded, and some was lost when its mission ended. Telling these cases apart lets us tell each story accurately, and shows how every mission builds on the ones before it.
